# Evaluating a design workflow

Use when the user asks whether the research/preflight workflow improves design or what it costs. Keep this record in task storage alongside evidence. Agree on the task and evaluation scope from the brief; a single pilot can describe useful findings and cost without establishing general effectiveness.

## Record during work

Keep a short ledger with UTC start/end timestamps for baseline inspection, reference research, design/prototype work, critique/corrections, and verification. Record meaningful iterations and their reason. Mark overlapping intervals and report end-to-end elapsed time separately; overlapping phase durations are not additive. Separate user/reviewer waits and tool time only when instrumentation supports it. Mark missing measurements unknown rather than reconstructing precise timings from memory. If evaluation starts mid-task, record that boundary and qualify earlier timing and causal claims.

At each design decision, identify the references already inspected, the observations that influenced a change or deliberate retention, and unresolved assumptions. Sources inspected later are corroboration unless they cause a recorded revision. Record both distinct examples and products represented; several useful examples from one product can answer different questions without providing independent product diversity.

For each critique finding, record its target artifact/revision, observed evidence versus hypothesis/preference, whether it was accepted or rejected and why, the correction if any, and verification result. Keep existing-product defects, proposed design benefits, and defects introduced by the prototype distinguishable. Note whether the author, an independent critic, or actual readers performed the check. Model agreement alone is not reader-outcome evidence.

## Assess separately

Reference relevance: which consequential questions were answered, what each source actually influenced, important mismatches, and whether additional searching added useful evidence. Evaluate contribution rather than meeting a count.

Critique quality: useful decisions retained, justified and rejected findings, repairs verified, and consequences for other tasks. Distinguish a working prototype from evidence that its proposed change improves the product. State when false-positive rates or reader benefits remain unknown.

Overhead: observed elapsed time, phase intervals, correction iterations, waits where measured, and unmeasured costs. Report unnecessary searching, simulation/debugging, and recapture costs when observed; their existence does not establish that the skills caused them.

Only claim comparative speed or quality improvements with a comparable baseline: equivalent task, content, constraints, and clearly described assessment. For reader outcomes, use appropriate task testing and report participants/method and limitations. Without that comparison, describe this run's costs and useful catches, not causal improvement. Recommend keeping, narrowing, or changing specific workflow steps with uncertainty beside the recommendation.

## Calibration checks

A fourth reference inspected after the prototype can enrich the evidence but cannot retroactively explain the initial decision. A narrow prototype compared with a wide product screenshot leaves the narrow redesign benefit unverified. Repairing a clipped pager created by the prototype verifies the repair, not superiority over the product. Use these hypothetical cases to calibrate evidence boundaries.
